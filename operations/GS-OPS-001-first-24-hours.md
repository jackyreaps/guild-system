# GS-OPS-001 — First 24 Hours Emergency Protocol

## 1. Purpose

When a member is targeted by a hostile state — detained, threatened, subjected to a travel ban, an asset freeze, or a coordinated disinformation campaign — the first 24 hours determine whether the response is effective or makes things worse. This Protocol specifies the steps.

It is the operational arm of the Chamber's emergency authority (GS-CHR-006 §10). Emergency authority expires after 72 hours unless extended.

## 2. Trigger

The Protocol activates when any of the following is reported by a member, peer, family member, or partner organization:

- Detention or arrest.
- Physical threat or violence.
- Travel ban or refusal of entry.
- Asset freeze or banking exclusion.
- Coordinated disinformation attack.
- Sustained surveillance or harassment.
- Forced disclosure of legal identity.

Any member may report. The report goes to the Ombuds (GS-CHR-006 §4.1) and to the Chamber.

## 3. The Twelve Steps

### Step 1 — Verify (0–2 hours)

Confirm the report through at least two independent channels. Avoid acting on unverified information. Do not publish anything until verification is complete.

### Step 2 — Safety Check (0–4 hours)

Ensure the member and their family are physically safe. If not, activate local counsel and partner organizations immediately.

### Step 3 — Activate Local Counsel (0–4 hours)

Contact counsel in the relevant jurisdiction. The member must not speak to authorities without counsel. Counsel is retained via the Counsel Network (GS-CHR-006 §5.9).

### Step 4 — Preserve Evidence (0–6 hours)

Securely preserve all communications, legal documents, threats, and records of the incident. Do not alter or delete anything. Evidence preservation is counsel-directed.

### Step 5 — Chamber Activation (0–6 hours)

Notify the Chamber. The Chamber convenes an emergency panel (quorum: five members, three jurisdictions). The panel assumes emergency authority under GS-CHR-006 §10.

### Step 6 — Risk Assessment (2–8 hours)

The Chamber rates the threat: low, medium, high, or extreme. The rating informs subsequent steps. Extreme risk triggers the Sovereign Relocation Protocol (GS-CHR-001 §5.1).

### Step 7 — Defense Fund Activation (4–12 hours)

If counsel is required and the member cannot afford it, the Chamber releases Defense Fund reserves under published rules (GS-CHR-006 §5.4). Disbursement requires Chamber approval and follows GS-OPS-005 §6.

### Step 8 — NGO and UN Engagement (6–18 hours)

Notify partner organizations as appropriate:

- Amnesty International
- Human Rights Watch
- Article 19
- Committee to Protect Journalists (if applicable)
- UN Special Rapporteurs (freedom of expression, arbitrary detention, human rights defenders)

The Chamber coordinates. The member decides whether to be named.

### Step 9 — Public Transparency (12–24 hours)

If safe, publish a factual statement:

- The member's moniker (not legal identity unless the member consents).
- What is known (verified facts only).
- What is being done (counsel, Chamber, partner organizations).
- What the public can do (petitions, contact representatives, public statement).

Do not publish speculation. Do not publish information that could endanger the member or family.

### Step 10 — Relocation (if needed, 12–48 hours)

If the risk is extreme, activate the Sovereign Relocation Protocol. This involves:

- Emergency travel documents (via partner organizations).
- Safe passage coordination.
- Temporary lodging in a safe jurisdiction.
- Ongoing legal representation.

The Chamber does not itself provide immigration advice. It coordinates with partner organizations.

### Step 11 — Sustain (24 hours onward)

The emergency phase ends at 72 hours. The ongoing defense is coordinated by the Chamber under normal authority. The member is assigned a case lead.

### Step 12 — After-Action Review (7–30 days)

The Chamber conducts an after-action review. What was the trigger? What worked? What failed? What should be changed in this Protocol? The review is published (subject to member safety).

## 4. Authority Limits

Emergency authority under GS-CHR-006 §10 is limited to:

- Activating local counsel.
- Releasing Defense Fund reserves within published limits.
- Notifying partner organizations.
- Publishing a factual statement if safe.

It does not extend to:

- Directing the member's legal strategy.
- Publishing information the member has not consented to publish.
- Committing Defense Fund resources beyond published limits.
- Making public statements that could endanger the member.

## 5. Communication Discipline

- All communication is encrypted (GS-OPS-002).
- No member discusses the case with anyone outside the Chamber, counsel, and the member's designated contacts.
- Social media is not used for case updates without Chamber approval.
- Journalists are engaged through partner organizations, not directly.
