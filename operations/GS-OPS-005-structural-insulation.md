# GS-OPS-005 — Structural Insulation Protocol

## Boundary Statement

This document defines structural requirements for legal entities. For drift detection, see **GS-CHR-005**. For reserve distribution thresholds, see **GS-OPS-006**.

## 1. Purpose

To specify the structural architecture that prevents any single actor — state, financial network, corporation, or captured institution — from liquidating the Guild System by attacking a visible target. The historical record shows a recurring pattern: concentrated interests capture state power, convert a debtor-creditor or jurisdictional relationship into a weapon, and destroy the visible counterparty to clear the field.

## 2. Definitions

- **Tier 1 — The Compact.** The shared protocol layer. No legal personality, no assets, no accounts, no address.
- **Tier 2 — Chartered Guilds.** Legal entities that sign contracts, hold accounts, employ members, appear on filings.
- **Tier 3 — Circles and Forge Cells.** Working units that produce artifacts. May be incorporated or unincorporated.
- **Counterparty.** Any entity the Guild System transacts with.
- **Intermediary.** A separate legal entity that holds a relationship on behalf of a guild.
- **Disclosure Boundary.** The line between lawful insulation and unlawful concealment.

## 3. The Three-Tier Architecture

**Tier 1 — The Compact.** Holds nothing. Signs nothing. Cannot be served. Carries the eight foundational rules.

**Tier 2 — Chartered Guilds.** Carry all contracts, accounts, and employment. Each is its own legal person. No guild binds the Compact. No guild binds another guild.

**Tier 3 — Circles and Forge Cells.** Produce artifacts. Liability limited to specific work. A circle's failure does not propagate upward.

**Insulation principle:** the higher the tier, the fewer the liabilities.

## 4. Counterparty Rules

No guild may be the direct counterparty to a state, sovereign entity, sovereign wealth fund, or large institution whose failure, hostility, or capture could damage the guild's core assets or operations.

If such a relationship is necessary, it routes through an intermediary. The guild is never the visible face of the relationship.

## 5. Intermediary Structures

Permitted:

- A separate operating subsidiary with its own legal personality.
- A partner organization — non-profit, cooperative, foundation — that holds the contract.
- A consortium or syndicate of member guilds, with shared and diffuse liability.
- A trust or holding structure with beneficial ownership disclosed on lawful request.

Choice is per-guild, per-jurisdiction, per-counterparty. The rule is: no direct sovereign exposure.

## 6. Disclosure Boundary

**Transparent:** beneficial ownership to lawful authorities on request; tax filings; regulatory compliance; the existence of the structure.

**Prohibited:** concealed ownership; undisclosed beneficial interests; structures whose purpose is defeating a lawful obligation; any arrangement that fails "if a lawful authority asks who ultimately benefits, the answer is available."

Insulation protects against hostile attack. It does not protect against lawful inquiry. If the structure cannot pass lawful inquiry, it is not insulation — it is concealment, and it is prohibited.

## 7. Jurisdictional Distribution

No guild may hold assets, records, members, and legal personality in a single jurisdiction.

- Assets in at least two jurisdictions.
- Records in at least two jurisdictions.
- Legal personality in a rule-of-law jurisdiction.
- Membership distributed across at least two jurisdictions.

The Compact does not name specific jurisdictions. It requires distribution as a structural property.

## 8. Anti-Concentration

Cross-references GS-OPS-006 §4. No guild may derive more than a defined share of funding, compute, or legal protection from a single source. The threshold is set per guild and reviewed by counsel. Exceeding it triggers the Drift Remedy Ladder (GS-CHR-005 §4).

## 9. Prohibited Structures

- Concealed ownership or beneficial interests.
- Sanctions evasion.
- Unlicensed money transmission.
- Wage substitution designed to defeat employment or tax law.
- Any structure whose purpose is defeating a lawful obligation.
- Any structure that cannot survive lawful inquiry.
