# GS-CHR-007 — Cross-Guild Dispute Resolution Charter

## 1. Purpose

Guilds will disagree. They will disagree about merit attestations, about fork boundaries, about membership claims, about artifact attribution, and about which guild is responsible for a shared liability. This Charter establishes how those disputes are resolved without a central authority, without destroying either guild, and without collapsing the Compact.

The Chamber (GS-CHR-006) does not adjudicate between guilds. This document fills that gap.

## 2. Scope

In scope:
- Disputes between two or more chartered guilds in good standing.
- Disputes between a guild and a member of another guild.
- Disputes about merit attestation recognition across guilds.
- Disputes about fork boundaries and asset division.
- Disputes about artifact attribution and provenance.

Out of scope:
- Disputes between a guild and a state (that is GS-CHR-006).
- Internal guild governance disputes (that is the guild's own charter).
- Criminal matters (those go to the relevant jurisdiction's courts).

## 3. Principles

1. **No central authority.** No body adjudicates. The process produces a resolution that both parties agree to, or it does not produce one.
2. **Both guilds retain autonomy.** A resolution does not bind a guild that did not consent to it.
3. **Fork is always available.** If a dispute cannot be resolved, the parties may separate under GS-CHR-008.
4. **Public process.** The existence of a dispute, the mediation steps, and the outcome are published, subject to member safety.
5. **Proportional.** The process must cost less than the dispute.

## 4. The Mediation Ladder

### Step 1 — Direct Contact

The parties attempt direct resolution within 14 days. No records required beyond the date of contact.

### Step 2 — Peer Mediation

If direct contact fails, either party may request a peer mediator. The mediator is a member of a third guild, chosen by mutual agreement or by lot from a published pool. Mediation lasts up to 30 days.

### Step 3 — Panel Review

If mediation fails, either party may request a panel review. The panel consists of three members from three guilds other than the parties. The panel hears both sides, reviews evidence, and issues a non-binding recommendation within 60 days.

### Step 4 — Public Record

If the panel recommendation is not accepted by both parties, the dispute and both positions are published in the public record. Each guild's reputation in the network is its own to defend.

### Step 5 — Separation

If the dispute concerns a shared liability, a fork boundary, or an unresolvable attribution question, either party may invoke GS-CHR-008. Fork is not failure; it is the resolution of last resort.

## 5. What the Process Does Not Do

- It does not issue binding judgments.
- It does not impose penalties.
- It does not override either guild's charter.
- It does not create a precedent that binds future disputes.

The process produces a recommendation and a public record. Guilds that ignore both lose standing in the network — through peer attestation, through recognition decisions, through reputation — but are not expelled by anyone.

## 6. Merit Attestation Disputes

If a guild disputes another guild's merit attestation of a shared artifact:

1. Both guilds publish their attestations with verification evidence.
2. A peer panel reviews both.
3. The panel recommends which attestation is better supported by evidence, or recommends recording both with a note on the dispute.
4. Each guild may accept or reject the recommendation.

No attestation is invalidated by a dispute. It is only contextualized.

## 7. Fork Boundary Disputes

If a guild forks and the two resulting entities disagree about which owns which artifacts, which members belong where, and which obligations carry:

1. The founding guild's Fork and Secession Protocol (GS-CHR-008) governs, unless both parties agree otherwise.
2. Disputed items go to peer panel review.
3. Unresolved disputes may result in dual attribution ("fork-of X" noted on the artifact).
4. Each guild continues to operate. Neither is dissolved.

## 8. Cross-Guild Liability

If a guild incurs a legal liability that implicates another guild (e.g., a shared contract, a joint artifact, a member who is in both guilds):

1. The liability is apportioned per the original contract or artifact attribution.
2. If apportionment is unclear, peer panel review recommends a split.
3. Each guild remains solely liable for its own share. The Compact does not create joint liability across guilds.

## 9. Publication and Privacy

Disputes are published in the public record per §4 Step 4, with member identities protected (monikers only). Evidence containing PII or legally sensitive material is summarized, not reproduced.
