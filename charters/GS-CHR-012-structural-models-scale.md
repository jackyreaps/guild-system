# GS-CHR-012 — Structural Models for Scale

Specifies three structural models by which a network of guilds can achieve durable scale: the Convocation Model (republican, consensus-based, fork-preserving), the Imperium Model (senatorial class, expansionist, earned-class), and the Sovereignty Model (kingship, valor-legitimated, lineage-coherent). Each is fully specified. Each has costs and benefits. The network chooses which model to operate under, and may transition between them under defined conditions.

The core principle: **scale is a structural problem with three valid historical answers. A network that specifies only the republican answer has specified only the model that fragments.**

## 1. Purpose

A network of autonomous guilds is a strong architecture for resilience and a weak architecture for action. When the network faces decisions with real consequences — a member detained by a hostile state, a shared protocol that needs a breaking change, a coordinated attack on three guilds at once — there must be a body with authority to decide and capacity to execute.

Three structural forms have produced durable scale in the historical record:

**The republican form.** Venice, the Dutch Republic, the Swiss Confederation, the early Roman Republic. A sovereign assembly, an executive with bounded authority, a ceremonial head. Scales through consensus. Preserves exit rights. Slower to decide. Fragments when consensus fails.

**The imperial form.** Rome under the Principate, the British Empire, the Venetian maritime empire, the Hanseatic League at its height. A dominant center, an earned class, a periphery that submits on defined matters. Scales through concentrated decision. Fast. Durable. Curtails exit rights. Concentrates authority in a class that can become hereditary and then brittle.

**The sovereign form.** Egypt under the Pharaohs, Persia under the Achaemenids, China under the dynasties, France under the Capetians, England under the Plantagenets and Tudors. A single control head, legitimated by valor and lineage, preserving coherence across generations. Scales through continuity of belief-line. Deeply durable. Curtails exit rights. Concentrates authority in one person whose competence is not guaranteed by the mechanism.

All three are real. All three have produced durable structures. All three have failure modes. This document specifies all three, so that the network can choose — and so that the choice is made deliberately rather than by drift.

The guild system does not require any model. It requires that the chosen model be specified, consented to by the guilds subject to it, and revocable if it fails.

## 2. The Question at Scale

At one guild, the question does not arise. At ten, coordination is informal. At a hundred, patterns emerge. At a thousand, without specified structures, patterns become structures, and structures become authorities no one decided to create.

**Failure by fragmentation.** Consensus bodies that cannot concentrate decision lose to networks that can.

**Failure by capture.** Earned classes that become hereditary ossify and lose legitimacy.

**Failure by decoherence.** A monarchical center whose head lacks valor loses the belief-line the model existed to preserve.

## 3. The Correction on Diversity

**Plurality** — multiple guilds, domains, and approaches coherent under shared protocols — is an asset.

**Fragmentation** — multiple centers with no phase-lock and no capacity for collective action — is a liability.

Convocation preserves plurality and manages fragmentation through consensus until consensus fails. Imperium prevents fragmentation by concentrating decision, at the cost of curtailing exit. Sovereignty prevents fragmentation by concentrating decision in a single head, at the cost of absolutizing exit within the network.

## 4. Model A — The Convocation

### 4.1 The Convocation (sovereign assembly)

Membership by published threshold: tenure (e.g. three years chartered in good standing); peer attestation (at least three peers); distribution compliance (GS-OPS-006); disclosure boundary (GS-OPS-005); no active Drift Remedy finding; aggregate merit above a floor set by the Convocation.

Powers: Compact amendment (two-thirds); breaking protocol changes; threshold amendments; Council and Steward elections and recall; override of Council decisions by simple majority.

### 4.2 The Council of Peers (executive)

Seven to eleven members, elected by the Convocation. One-year terms, renewable once. Enumerated emergency and protocol powers. Cannot amend Compact or guild charters; cannot expel; cannot compel; cannot hold Defense Fund; cannot perform peer attestation; cannot bind non-consenting guilds; cannot delay a fork.

### 4.3 The Steward (ceremonial head)

Elected by the Convocation. Convenes, speaks, tie-break on procedure, signs protocols. Cannot command resources or issue Council decisions independently. Recallable by two-thirds.

**Naming note:** Network Steward is not the local rank label `steward` used in runtime Merit bands (RS-INF-003).

### 4.4 Costs and benefits

Provides: fork guarantee, consent, distributed resilience, plurality. Costs: slower decisions, fragmentation risk at extreme scale, weaker external presence.

## 5. Model B — The Imperium

### 5.1 The Senate (earned class)

Membership by class criteria: longer tenure; stronger attestation; top-decile aggregate merit sustained across cycles; full insulation/resilience compliance; class maintenance by continued contribution.

The Senate recognizes members; it does not “API-admit” them. Criteria are published and audited annually.

### 5.2 The Princeps

Elected by the Senate from its members. Executive authority over shared matters; emergency authority subject to Senate review. Recallable by two-thirds.

### 5.3 The Periphery

Guilds outside the Senate retain internal governance and charters; submit on foreign relations, defense, shared protocols, network disputes, and the Compact. Opt-in via Imperial Compact adoption.

### 5.4 Costs and benefits

Provides: durable scale, fast external action, earned class, institutional memory. Costs: curtailed fork at periphery, class degradation risk, concentration, legitimacy cost.

## 6. Model C — Sovereignty

### 6.1 The Sovereign

Legitimated by **valor** — demonstrated willingness to bear the cost of coherence for the whole, over time, under load — not by title alone and not by birth alone.

Valor tests include: cost borne; coherence maintained under load; faithfulness to the belief-line; willingness to be judged.

### 6.2 Lineage and succession

Lineage preserves a belief-line across generations. Succession is not automatic: the successor must demonstrate valor before accession.

### 6.3 Council of Valor

Audits valor annually; can declare valor not demonstrated and trigger Succession Review. Does not elect the sovereign day-to-day.

### 6.4 Costs and benefits

Provides: deep continuity, single decision center, fragmentation resistance, valor as legitimating virtue. Costs: no internal network fork, succession risk, concentration, no republican consent of the governed.

## 7. Safeguards Common to All Three Models

Revocation, sunset where applicable, transparency of decisions, exit from the network, fork rules as model-specific, annual audit of earned class (Imperium) and valor (Sovereignty).

## 8. The Transition

Specified paths To Imperium, To Sovereignty, To Convocation, and concurrent models on different layers connected by GS-CHR-009 — without one body commanding another across models.

## 9. Historical Record

Republican, imperial, and sovereign successes and failure modes as previously drafted (Venice, Dutch Republic, Swiss Confederation, Hanse; Rome, British Empire; Egypt, China, Persia, France, England). All three produce durable scale; none is universally superior.

## 10. What No Model Is

None is a territorial state. None is permanent. None is required — a guild may remain external and participate per attestation only.

## 11. The Principle, Stated Once

A network that cannot decide cannot act. A network that cannot act cannot protect its members. Specifying all three models makes the choice deliberate, consent-based, and reversible. Valor, earned class, and consensus each fail if they stop being demonstrated and become mere inheritance or empty procedure.

## 12. Portfolio Bridge — Ranks, Titles, and Valor Records

This section links **local runtime evidence** to **network representation** without collapsing the runtime into authority.

### 12.1 Layers

| Layer | Definition | Where specified |
|-------|------------|-----------------|
| Merit | Permanent verified contribution score | GS-CHR-002 |
| Rank | Local Merit-band label inside one guild | Local Charter (GS-CHR-010); runtime RS-INF-003 |
| Title | Named, non-purchasable achievement bound to a moniker | Local Charter; runtime RS-INF-004 |
| Valor record | Restricted evidence family: cost borne, coherence under load, belief-line, judgment accepted | This charter §6; runtime RS-INF-004 |
| Portfolio | Rank + titles + valor events for one moniker in one guild | Runtime store (e.g. Resileos hubs + ledger) |
| Representation | Seat, class recognition, or sovereign charge under this charter | GS-CHR-012 bodies only |

### 12.2 Rules

1. Titles and valor are earned, not purchased. Credits never buy titles, ranks, or valor status.
2. Narrative valor may be recorded as **pending**; **accomplished** requires attestation or published audit evidence.
3. A rich portfolio (many titles, repeated accomplished valor) is **eligibility and evidence** for higher-order representation. It does **not** auto-appoint Council member, Senate seat, Steward, Princeps, or Sovereign.
4. Local rank name `steward` is not the Convocation Steward office.
5. Runtime systems (RS-INF-001 boundary) store portfolios and work boards; they do not perform peer attestation, hold the Defense Fund, or adjudicate.

### 12.3 Model-specific use of portfolios

| Model | How portfolios are used |
|-------|-------------------------|
| **Convocation** | Optional published criteria may require minimum rank, title set, or attestation count for candidacy or seating — still subject to election / threshold rules in §4 |
| **Imperium** | Title portfolio and sustained Merit contribute to *earned-class evidence*; Senate recognition remains a political act under §5, not a ledger trigger |
| **Sovereignty** | Accomplished valor records are **inputs** to the Valor Audit (§6.4); Succession Review may inspect portfolios; valor is demonstrated in public contest, not granted by API |

### 12.4 Work boards and bounties

Guild-local work boards (quests/bounties) produce Merit, Credits, and optionally title recommendations on completion. Federation-scale emergencies remain under GS-OPS-001 and the bodies of the active model — not under a single guild board.

### 12.5 Scale path

Tens of members: one guild, ranks, board. Hundreds: circles shard boards; titles mark specialists. Thousands: many chartered guilds; GS-CHR-009 interop; portfolios feed representation under the model(s) the network has consented to.

## 14. Runtime boundary (normative for implementers)

If a proposed feature would: attest compliance as a peer; hold or move Defense Fund assets; adjudicate disputes; classify legal demands; appoint network officers by database mutation alone; or execute a legal fork — it is **not** a conforming runtime feature under this charter. Portfolios inform humans and bodies; bodies decide.
