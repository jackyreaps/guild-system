# GS-CHR-006 — Juridical Chamber Charter

## 1. Purpose

To establish the body that assesses legal demands made against the Guild System, coordinates defense, protects members engaged in lawful public-interest work, and maintains the legitimacy standard that governs the system's relationship with state authority.

The Chamber operates across many jurisdictions with varying legal protections. Its function is to distinguish legitimate demands from predatory ones, respond proportionally, and preserve the system's legitimacy under scrutiny. It is not a court. It does not adjudicate disputes between guilds (that is GS-CHR-007). It does not override any jurisdiction's legal process.

## 2. Definitions

- **Legitimate Legal Demand.** A request satisfying all seven criteria of the Legitimacy Test (§6). A demand can be lawful under local statute and still fail the test if the law itself is predatory.
- **Predatory Request.** A demand failing any criterion of the Legitimacy Test.
- **Hostile State.** As defined in GS-CHR-001 §7.
- **Member.** Any individual participating in a chartered guild under a moniker.
- **Guild Work.** Public or authorized contribution produced under a guild charter.
- **Counsel Network.** The distributed group of legal professionals retained or consulted by the Chamber across jurisdictions.
- **Defense Fund.** The charter-governed mutual aid pool for members facing retaliation, prosecution, or civil suits for lawful public-interest work.

## 3. Mandate

The Chamber is authorized to:

1. Assess the legitimacy of legal demands against guilds or members.
2. Coordinate legal defense when members face retaliation for lawful work.
3. Administer the Defense Fund under published rules.
4. Maintain and expand the Counsel Network.
5. Advise guilds on compliance with lawful obligations.
6. Publish transparency reports on legal demands, takedowns, and state pressure.
7. Preserve the legitimacy standard across jurisdictions as legal conditions change.

The Chamber is **not** authorized to override a court's lawful order, bind a guild to a legal position without consent, direct members to violate a lawful obligation, investigate members' private affairs, or act as prosecutor, judge, or enforcer within the Guild System.

## 4. Composition

### 4.1 Standing Members

- Rotating counsel from the Counsel Network, cross-jurisdictional.
- Ethics panel of three to five members drawn from the broader Guild membership.
- Two member advocates elected by the Assembly of Charters.
- Two independent observers from recognized human-rights organizations.
- One ombuds, elected by the Assembly, for internal disputes only.

### 4.2 Terms

- Counsel rotate every 12 months.
- Ethics panel rotates every 18 months.
- Member advocates serve 12 months, renewable once.
- Independent observers serve 24 months.
- Ombuds serves 24 months, not renewable consecutively.

### 4.3 Quorum and Decision Rules

- Quorum: five members, at least three jurisdictions represented.
- Routine determinations: simple majority.
- Emergency determinations: two-thirds majority.
- Constitutional questions: unanimous, referred to Assembly.

## 5. Functions

### 5.1 Pre-Publication Legal Review

Every artifact published under a guild's name passes review for defamation, trade-secret, computer-misuse, privacy, and export-control risk.

### 5.2 Legitimacy Assessment

Every legal demand is assessed under §6 and classified under §8.

### 5.3 Defense Coordination

When a member faces retaliation for lawful work, the Chamber activates local counsel, preserves evidence, and coordinates public response.

### 5.4 Defense Fund Administration

The Chamber releases funds under published rules. Disbursement requires Chamber approval and follows the disclosure requirements of GS-OPS-005 §6.

### 5.5 Asylum and Relocation Support

The Chamber coordinates with partner organizations to provide relocation support when a member faces physical threat. It does not itself provide legal immigration advice.

### 5.6 Transparency Reporting

An annual report listing legal demands received, classifications applied, actions taken, and outcomes where disclosable without endangering members.

### 5.7 Dispute Resolution and Correction

The Chamber administers the correction and right-of-reply process for published artifacts. It does not adjudicate disputes between guilds (GS-CHR-007).

### 5.8 Legal Literacy Training

Training materials for members on local rights, the Legitimacy Test, and emergency procedures (GS-OPS-001).

### 5.9 Counsel Network Maintenance

Recruits, vets, and coordinates with counsel across jurisdictions. Counsel relationships are disclosed under §12.

## 6. The Legitimacy Test

Every demand is assessed against seven criteria. Each produces a finding of **satisfied**, **not satisfied**, or **indeterminate**.

### 6.1 Legal Basis

Is there a valid law supporting the demand? Is the law itself consistent with international human-rights standards? A demand can rest on a statute and still fail this criterion if the statute is predatory.

### 6.2 Due Process

Was proper procedure followed? Notification, access to counsel, hearing, uniform application.

### 6.3 Proportionality

Is the demand proportionate to the alleged wrong? Does it target more than necessary?

### 6.4 Jurisdiction

Does the requesting authority have lawful jurisdiction?

### 6.5 Human Rights Compliance

Would compliance violate the member's fundamental rights — expression, association, privacy, assembly, due process?

### 6.6 Targeting of Lawful Work

Does the demand target lawful public-interest work — public artifacts, published proofs, open-source contributions, civic provision?

### 6.7 Absence of Pretext

Is the stated reason the actual reason? Are there indicators that the real motive is suppression of lawful work? Retroactive application, targeted legislation, procedural traps, and selective enforcement are pretext indicators.

## 7. Decision Process

1. **Intake.** Report filed.
2. **Conflict Check.** Among standing members.
3. **Legal Analysis.** Jurisdiction-specific analysis by Counsel Network.
4. **Human-Rights Assessment.** By independent observers.
5. **Risk Rating.** Low, medium, high, extreme.
6. **Determination.** Classification under §8.
7. **Appeal.** Under §9.

## 8. The Demand Determination Scale

This framework applies exclusively to external jurisdictional mandates and cannot be substituted or cross-applied to the Drift Remedy Ladder (GS-CHR-005 §4), which governs internal guild governance.

### 8.1 Comply

Legitimate. Full compliance.

### 8.2 Comply in Part

Some elements legitimate, some not. Comply with legitimate elements; challenge or resist the others through lawful means.

### 8.3 Challenge

Questionable. Contest through legal channels — motions to quash, appeals, constitutional challenges — without exposing members to unnecessary risk.

### 8.4 Publicize

Predatory. Document, publish a factual account where safe, notify human-rights organizations and, where appropriate, UN mechanisms.

### 8.5 Resist

Unlawful. Do not comply. Provide legal defense, activate Defense Fund, coordinate public response through lawful channels. Requires two-thirds majority and automatic review after 30 days.

## 9. Appeals

Any affected member or guild may appeal within 30 days. Appeals heard by a panel of Ethics Panel and Independent Observers, excluding original participants. Simple majority.

## 10. Emergency Authority

When a member is detained, threatened, or subjected to a travel ban or asset freeze, the Chamber may act under GS-OPS-001. Emergency authority is limited to:

- Activating local counsel.
- Releasing Defense Fund reserves within published limits.
- Notifying partner organizations.
- Publishing a factual statement if safe.

Emergency authority expires after 72 hours unless extended by a two-thirds majority.

## 11. Transparency

The Chamber publishes:

- An annual transparency report.
- Classifications of legal demands, aggregated to protect member privacy.
- Outcomes of appeals.
- Updates to Counsel Network and Defense Fund rules.
- Corrections to any published statement.

No identifying member information is published without consent, except where required by a lawful order the Chamber has been unable to challenge.

## 12. Conflict of Interest

Standing members disclose financial relationships, employment, and prior legal representation of any party to a matter. Disclosure is made to the Ombuds, who determines whether recusal is required. Recusal decisions are published.
