# GS-CHR-004 — Civic Substitution & Mutual Provision Annex

## 1. Purpose

The Guild System does not avoid obligations. Where public goods are unfunded, corrupted, or denied, voluntary mutual institutions may provide them in parallel. This Annex specifies the recognized public-good functions, the provision mechanisms, and the Tax-Integrity Floor that separates lawful provision from unlawful evasion.

## 2. The Principle

Taxes exist to fund public goods. Where those goods are not funded, voluntary mutual institutions may provide them. Parallel provision is lawful. Concealment of income is not. The Guild System provides at the point of service, not at the point of obligation.

## 3. The Tax-Integrity Floor

- All members meet lawful tax obligations in their jurisdictions.
- No Guild structure conceals income, mischaracterizes compensation, or defeats a lawful obligation.
- Guild Credits route *surplus contribution* toward public-good functions, never *around* a legal duty.
- Every guild files a local civic-provision annex reviewed by counsel.

A friendly society that funds a school and a person who hides income to avoid funding a school are worlds apart in law. The distinction is not negotiable.

## 4. Recognized Public-Good Functions

The Compact sets this floor. Guilds may expand; may not subtract.

- Education and literacy
- Health and mental health support
- Local infrastructure and logistics
- Emergency response
- Legal aid and rights education
- Science and civic literacy
- Care work

## 5. Provision Mechanisms

- Non-convertible credits redeemable only for mutual goods
- Mutual credit among members for service exchange
- Cooperative and friendly-society structures
- Non-profit and educational charters
- Community land and tool trusts
- Compute and hardware sharing cooperatives

## 6. Legal Bases

- Cooperative and mutual society law
- Non-profit and educational entity law
- Charitable and mutual-aid statutes
- Time-bank and LETS precedents
- Religious and fraternal benefit society law

## 7. Historical Precedents

- **Friendly societies** (UK, 18th–20th c.) — working-class mutuals providing health, burial, unemployment, education before the welfare state
- **Credit unions and mutuals** — member-owned finance, legally recognized worldwide
- **Mondragon cooperatives** (Spain) — integrated education, health, industry under cooperative law
- **Kibbutzim** — mutual provision of education, care, infrastructure
- **ROSCAs and burial societies** — rotating savings, mutual insurance across many cultures
- **LETS and time banks** — modern mutual credit, often legally non-taxable

These are the models. GS-REF-001 presents the cautionary case.

## 8. Prohibited Structures

- Concealed income
- Wage substitution designed to defeat employment or tax law
- Self-dealing through charitable wrappers
- Anonymous high-value transfers
- Any structure whose purpose is defeating a lawful obligation
